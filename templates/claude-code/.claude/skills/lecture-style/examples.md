# Examples for the lecture-style skill

One pair per rule in SKILL.md: a passage that breaks the rule and the corrected version. The pairs below are placeholders from an asset-pricing module. Replace them with passages from your own material (chapter 13, step 4), keeping one pair for each rule you rely on.

## Rule 1: one idea per slide, and a title that states the point

Breaks rule 1:   title "Portfolio sorts"
Corrected:       title "Sorting on book-to-market spreads average returns across portfolios"

Breaks rule 1:   one slide that sets out the CAPM, the first-pass time-series regression and the second-pass cross-sectional regression
Corrected:       three slides, one per step, each with a title that states what that step estimates

## Rule 2: paragraphs in notes are at most four sentences

Breaks rule 2:   a six-sentence paragraph that derives the OLS estimator, discusses its bias with a lagged dependent variable and introduces the next section
Corrected:       one paragraph for the derivation, one for the bias, and the link to the next section moved to the start of that section

## Rule 3: number only the equations that are used later

Breaks rule 3:   every displayed equation numbered, including intermediate steps of a derivation that are never referred to again
Corrected:       only the moment condition and the estimator are numbered, because later slides refer to them as (1) and (2)

## Rule 4: define a symbol in the sentence where it first appears

Breaks rule 4:   "The price of risk is estimated by regressing r_it on beta_i." (r_it and beta_i are defined two slides later)
Corrected:       "The price of risk is estimated by regressing r_it, the excess return on portfolio i in month t, on beta_i, its estimated market beta."

## Rule 5: follow each result with one sentence of interpretation

Breaks rule 5:   "The estimated price of market risk is not significantly different from zero." (the slide ends there)
Corrected:       "The estimated price of market risk is not significantly different from zero. In this sample, portfolios with higher market betas did not earn higher average returns."

## Rule 6: worked examples use real data and state the source and sample period

Breaks rule 6:   "Suppose a stock has an expected return of 8% and a beta of 1.2."
Corrected:       "Monthly returns on the 25 size and book-to-market portfolios from Kenneth French's data library, July 1963 to December 2024."

## Do not

Announcing:      "In this section we will look at how to test the CAPM."
Corrected:       start with the first step of the test

Question title:  title "Does beta explain returns?"
Corrected:       title "Beta does not explain the cross-section of average returns in this sample"

Listed phrase:   "It is important to note that the standard errors must be corrected."
Corrected:       "The standard errors must be corrected for the estimated betas in the first pass."
